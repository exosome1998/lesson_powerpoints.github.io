Evidence for Evolution

Year 10 Science • embryology, homologous structures and fossils
Name:Date:
Big idea: Evolution is change in inherited characteristics of populations over generations. Scientists use different kinds of evidence to infer that living things share ancestors.

EVIDENCE 11. Comparative embryology

Embryology = the study of how embryos develop. Shared early features can suggest common ancestry.
Simple teaching sketches of vertebrate embryos at broadly comparable stages; not to scale. Coloured marks indicate pharyngeal arches, not functioning human gills.
IDENTIFY name them — Name TWO features shared by the three early embryos. (2 marks)
EXPLAIN link to why — Why do similar early structures support common ancestry? (2 marks)

LOOK2. Watch development change

As development continues, inherited body plans are modified in different ways. Similar does NOT mean identical.
COMPARE difference — State ONE difference between two later forms shown. (1 mark)
EXPLAIN — Why are early comparisons more useful than saying “all embryos look the same”? (2 marks)

EVIDENCE 23. Homologous forelimbs

These are homologous structures: body parts with a similar underlying arrangement because of shared ancestry, even if their jobs differ.
Schematics of forelimb bones; colours match corresponding bone groups. Not to scale.

Name:Class:

EVIDENCE 34. Fossil record

Fossils are preserved remains or traces of past organisms. In an undisturbed sequence, lower sedimentary rock layers are generally older.
Important: This is a simplified sequence of related groups — NOT proof that one pictured animal became the next.
LOOK CLOSELY: a transitional fossil
Tiktaalik lived about 375 million years ago. It had fish features AND robust limb-like fin bones. The fossil helps reveal evolutionary transitions.

USE THE DIAGRAM5. Fossil questions

IDENTIFY name — Which labelled fossil is in the middle layer? (1 mark)
DESCRIBE state feature — Where are the oldest fossils found in an undisturbed rock sequence? (1 mark)
OUTLINE two main points — Give TWO features of Tiktaalik that make it a transitional fossil. (2 marks)
EXPLAIN evidence → why — How does this fossil sequence support evolution over time? (3 marks)
A transitional fossil shows a mixture of characteristics seen in related groups. It need not be the direct ancestor of a living species.
