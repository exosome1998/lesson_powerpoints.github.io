1. Distance, speed and time

Speed describes how far an object travels in each second. Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.

2. Changing motion

A speed–time graph that slopes upwards shows increasing speed. A horizontal part shows constant speed. A downwards slope shows decreasing speed.

3. Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. In a straight line, acceleration = change in velocity ÷ elapsed time.
Calculate A scooter increases velocity from 3 to 15 m/s in 4 seconds.
Calculate A cart changes velocity from 2 m/s to 22 m/s over 5 s. Find acceleration.
Calculate A bus slows from 18 m/s to 6 m/s in 4 s. Find its acceleration (with sign).

4. Balanced forces

Forces are pushes and pulls. When total force is zero, the velocity does not change: an object stays still or continues at constant velocity.
Identify four forces shown on the box.
Predict motion if forward push balances friction and vertical forces balance.

5. Newton’s laws

A net force causes acceleration. A greater net force causes greater acceleration for a fixed mass, while a larger mass gives less acceleration for the same force.
Explain what happens to acceleration if force doubles and mass is unchanged.
Apply A net force of 18 N acts on a 6 kg trolley. Calculate acceleration.
Calculate A 5 kg trolley accelerates at 2.4 m/s². Find the net force.
Calculate A 32 N net force acts on an 8 kg crate. Find acceleration.

6. Graph interpretation

The area under a speed–time graph gives distance when speed is positive. Separate simple regions into rectangles or triangles.

Explain why a wider rectangle under a graph represents more distance.
Calculate A speed–time graph rises uniformly from 0 to 10 m/s over 6 s. Find the triangular area (distance).

7. Wave properties

A wave transfers energy. Amplitude is maximum displacement. Wavelength is the distance between matching points on neighbouring cycles.
Identify what λ labels on the wave diagram.
Describe how higher amplitude affects a water wave.

8. Frequency and wavelength

Frequency is the number of cycles passing a point each second. Wave speed = frequency × wavelength.
Calculate A wave has frequency 6 Hz and wavelength 2 m. Find speed.
Calculate Ripples travel at 15 m/s and have a frequency of 5 Hz. Calculate wavelength.
Calculate A wave travels at 24 m/s with wavelength 4 m. Calculate frequency.
Predict what happens to wavelength if frequency rises while wave speed stays constant.

9. Sound and particles

Sound needs a medium. Vibrations pass between particles in solids, liquids and gases; sound cannot travel through a vacuum.
Explain why sound cannot travel through empty space.

10. Reflection

A ray striking a flat reflecting surface bounces away. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, both measured from the normal.

11. Electromagnetic spectrum

Electromagnetic waves travel through a vacuum. The spectrum includes radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma radiation.

1. Chemical or physical?

A physical change alters a material without making a new substance. A chemical change produces one or more new substances.
Classify Melting wax and rusting steel as physical or chemical changes.

2. Conservation of atoms

In chemical reactions, atoms rearrange. In a closed system, no atoms are gained or lost, so total mass is conserved.
Describe what happens to the atoms in a reaction.
Explain why both sides of an equation need equal numbers of each atom.

Individuals of the same species can have different inherited traits. Environmental conditions may favour some traits over others.
