Marsden High School · Science 2027L16 · 08
R.E.A.D. · One designated literacy task

READ · Part 1

Long ago, many people used an Earth-centred model to explain the sky. In the second century CE, Ptolemy described a detailed geocentric model. In this model, Earth was at the centre and the Sun, Moon and planets moved around it. The model helped people predict positions in the sky.

In 1543, Nicolaus Copernicus published a heliocentric model. He placed the Sun near the centre of the planetary system and described Earth as one of the planets orbiting the Sun. Earth's daily rotation could explain the apparent daily movement of the sky.

Marsden High School · Science 2027L16 · 09
R.E.A.D. · One designated literacy task

READ · Part 2

In 1610, Galileo used a telescope to observe Jupiter. He saw small points of light whose positions changed over several nights. He concluded that they were moons orbiting Jupiter. This observation challenged the claim that every object in the sky orbited Earth. Other observations, including the phases of Venus, also supported changes to earlier models.

Galileo's evidence did not establish every detail of the modern model by itself. Later scientists collected more observations and improved explanations of planetary motion. The example shows how scientific models are tested, revised and sometimes replaced when new evidence becomes available.

Literacy / EAL/D

Scientific vocabulary

Solar eclipse
The Moon blocks sunlight from reaching part of Earth.
Lunar eclipse
The Moon moves into Earth's shadow.
Alignment
Objects arranged along a similar line.
Inclination
Tilt of an orbital plane.
Marsden High School · Science 2027L20 · 04
Explicit teaching · Step 1

Solar eclipse

Sun–Moon–Earth: the Moon's shadow reaches part of Earth.

Marsden High School · Science 2027L20 · 05
Explicit teaching · Step 2

Lunar eclipse

Sun–Earth–Moon: the Moon passes through Earth's shadow.

Marsden High School · Science 2027L20 · 06
Explicit teaching · Step 3

Compare alignments

Solar eclipses occur near New Moon; lunar eclipses occur near Full Moon.

Marsden High School · Science 2027L20 · 07
Explicit teaching · Step 4

The Moon's tilted orbit

Its orbit is tilted about 5° relative to Earth's orbital plane, so shadows often miss.

Marsden High School · Science 2027L20 · 08
Explicit teaching · Step 5

Phases are not eclipses

Normal phases occur regularly; eclipses need a special alignment.

Marsden High School · Science 2027L20 · 09
Explicit teaching · Step 6

Observe safely

Never look directly at the Sun without suitable certified solar viewing protection. Use teacher-approved indirect projection.
